Sewage in the house needs protective gear and careful removal. Here is what to do first, who to call and how cleanup works.

A sewage backup is wastewater coming up through a toilet, a tub, a shower or a floor drain. It is contaminated. The water can carry bacteria, and porous materials that touch it often cannot be saved. This is not a cleanup for a mop and a fan, and the crew works in protective gear.

Two parties are involved. A plumber or the sewer agency clears the blockage, and we remove the water and sanitize. Where the blockage is decides who is responsible, and that is why the first call is often a plumber.

What You See and Where the Problem May Be

What you seeWhere the blockage may beWho to call
One drain backs upThe branch line for that fixtureA plumber
Several fixtures back up at onceThe main private lateral to the streetA plumber, then the sewer agency if it is public
Toilet bubbles when the washer drainsA blockage in the shared lineA plumber
Wastewater at a floor drain or cleanoutA blocked lateral or mainA plumber and the sewer agency
Sewer smell with no visible waterA trap or vent problem or a hidden leakA plumber

The property owner maintains the private sewer lateral. The public line belongs to the sewer district.

Sewage Safety Steps While Water Is Spreading

Do these steps and keep it simple

  • ✓Stop using water in the house. Do not flush, run the washer or use sinks until the blockage is cleared, because each drain adds to the backup.
  • ✓Keep children and pets out of the area. Wash hands after any contact and do not wade through the water.
  • ✓Do not touch outlets, cords or the panel if the water is near them. Call 911 or your electric utility.
  • ✓Do not run household fans over sewage water. They spread contaminated droplets.
  • ✓Move items out of the splash zone only if you can do it without touching the water.
  • ✓Photograph what you see, then call a plumber and call us.

How Sewage Cleanup Works

1

Contain and protect

The crew wears protective gear and seals off the affected area so contamination does not travel.

2

Extract the wastewater

Pumps and extraction units remove the liquid. The work follows the category 3 water guidance in ANSI/IICRC S500, the published standard for water damage restoration.

3

Remove porous materials

Carpet, pad, insulation and drywall that touched the water are typically removed and bagged. Hard surfaces are cleaned and sanitized.

4

Sanitize and dry

Antimicrobial treatment is applied and the area is dried with air movers and dehumidifiers. Odor control follows.

What Moves the Cost

The main factors are how much area the wastewater reached, what the floors and walls are made of, how much must be removed and whether contents need cleaning or disposal. Equipment days and disposal add to it. Before work starts, we walk through the scope and the price.

Insurance Fit for Sewage Backups

Sewer and drain backups are often handled by a specific endorsement and are not always covered under a basic homeowners policy. Check your policy for backup language and call your agent. We cannot say what will be paid.

Keep the plumber's report that shows where the blockage was, along with your photos. The California Code of Regulations lets an insurer take 15 calendar days to acknowledge a claim and 40 calendar days to accept or deny it after proof of claim.

Mistakes to Avoid

Using a mop and bleach alone

Bleach does not make porous materials safe and can mix badly with other cleaners.

Flushing to test the line

Each flush can send more wastewater into the house.

Waiting for it to go down

Wastewater does not drain itself out of carpet and drywall.

Skipping the plumber

If the blockage is not cleared the backup returns.
