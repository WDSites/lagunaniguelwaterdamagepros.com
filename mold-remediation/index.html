Mold remediation is the process of containing mold-affected areas, removing contaminated materials, cleaning what can be saved and correcting the moisture that fed the growth. In Laguna Niguel it usually begins with a leak that went unnoticed or was dried too late. This page covers that cleanup, which is different from drying a fresh leak or rebuilding afterward.

Our process follows ANSI/IICRC S520, the published industry standard for mold remediation. We explain scope and price before work starts. Call (949) 670-0939 for a same-day response when available.

Musty smell, dark spots, or a stain that keeps coming back?

Those are signs of long-lasting moisture, and the stain or smell is the part you can see. Behind it, drywall paper, insulation or framing may still be damp. The trade that finds the moisture, contains the growth and removes it properly is mold remediation, and that is what the rest of this page explains.

Handle it yourself or call a crew

EPA says an area smaller than about 10 square feet, roughly a 3 foot by 3 foot patch, can usually be handled by the homeowner. It advises hiring help when there is a lot of water damage or growth covers more than that. Sewage or contaminated water calls for a professional with experience in that cleanup. Do not paint or caulk over mold, because the moisture underneath keeps working.

Mold Removal and Black Mold Cleanup

Dark or black growth gets the same careful process as any other color. Color alone cannot tell you the type of mold, so a crew treats it by what it is growing on, how far it has spread and how wet the material is. Porous items such as drywall, insulation and carpet that are mold-affected are usually removed. Hard surfaces such as framing and tile can often be cleaned.

The work is contained with plastic sheeting, and a negative air machine with a HEPA filter keeps air moving out through the filter instead of into the rest of your home. Materials are removed, bagged and carried out, then surfaces are HEPA vacuumed and damp wiped. Areas are dried and checked with moisture meters before anyone rebuilds.

Mold After Water Damage

Mold needs moisture. EPA says that if wet or damp materials are dried within 24 to 48 hours after a leak or spill, in most cases mold will not grow. That window is why a leak under a sink, behind a toilet or along an AC condensate line matters so much. Roof and siding leaks are worse, since the water often travels into walls where you cannot see it.

Check the cabinet under each sink and the wall behind each toilet first. Those are the spots where slow leaks hide longest.

If water just came into your home, structural drying is the first call, and it can prevent a later remediation job. If growth is already present, the cleanup comes first and any rebuild second. When the area is clean and dry, our water damage repair page covers replacing drywall, flooring and paint.

Mold Testing and Inspection

EPA states that in most cases, if visible mold growth is present, sampling is unnecessary. It also says no EPA or other federal limits have been set for mold or mold spores. For you, this means a test result will not give a pass or fail number, so testing is not a way to decide whether visible growth must be removed.

Surface sampling can be useful afterward, to determine whether an area has been adequately cleaned. Ask about independent clearance testing after remediation, meaning a tester who does not work for the cleanup company. A buyer's lender sometimes asks for that documentation. An inspection starts with moisture readings, a visual survey and questions about the leak history, and sampling is added only when there is a reason.

Safety before anyone starts on suspected mold

  • ✓Do not run a standard fan or HVAC across the area. It can spread disturbed material through the house.
  • ✓If water touched an electrical panel or outlets, stay out. Cut power only if the panel is dry and safe to reach, and call 911 or your electric utility first.
  • ✓If a drain backed up, treat the area as contaminated. Keep people and pets away and leave it to trained crews.
  • ✓If you smell gas, leave and call SoCalGas before anything else.
  • ✓Questions about symptoms belong with your physician.

Licenses, scope and what to ask a mold company

California's contractor board (CSLB) has no mold or water damage classification in its list. Ask whether your state licenses mold work and what license applies to your job. The rebuild trades, such as drywall, flooring, painting and plumbing, are licensed, and CSLB requires a license for work costing $1,000 or more in labor and materials combined. You can check a license on CSLB's License Check tool.

Ask any company whether its crews work to ANSI/IICRC S520. Get the scope in writing, including what will be removed, how the area is contained and how success is measured.
