October and November: the first rains find weak spots

NOAA's 1991 to 2020 normals for the Laguna Beach station, the nearest coastal record in our research, show about 0.49 inches of rain in October and 1.13 in November. After a dry summer, that first rain finds clogged gutters, cracked sealant and failed flashing. Check the roof edge and window trim before the first storm.

If a ceiling stain appears after that first rain, treat it as active. Put a container under it and call the line so a moisture meter can show how far it has spread.

December to March: San Juan Creek, Salt Creek and the coast

The same station averages 2.78 inches in January and 3.39 in February. Dana Point's draft Safety Element says San Juan Creek poses the greatest flood hazard to the city, with a floodplain 700 to 1,200 feet wide. It adds that the Army Corps finds the channel cannot hold a 100-year storm, and that the creek often breaks through the sand barrier at Doheny State Beach in winter.

Salt Creek runs through the Monarch Beach Golf Links, with a 100-year floodplain about 200 feet wide out to Pacific Coast Highway. If you live near either creek, look up your address on the FEMA flood map, because flood is usually excluded from standard homeowners policies.

Along the shore, FEMA maps a Coastal Flood with Velocity Hazard zone, and the city notes that storm run-up can exceed existing foundation heights. The Safety Element also encourages the Capistrano Bay community and the Niguel Shores HOA to keep sandbags on hand.

After the rain: bluffs, slopes and saturated ground

The Safety Element lists landslide and mudslide potential across Dana Point, with higher susceptibility along the coast north of the Harbor, west of Niguel Road, north of Stonehill Drive, and along Del Obispo Street, Doheny Park Road and Coast Highway. It calls Capistrano Bluffs an ongoing concern.

If you live on a bluff or slope, watch for new wet spots, cracks in retaining walls or doors that suddenly stick. Report them to the dispatcher with the water you can see, and ask your city building department what a repair on a slope needs.

May to September: dry skies, indoor leaks

The Laguna Beach normals fall below 0.25 inches every month from May through September. When a Dana Point ceiling or floor gets wet in summer, the cause is usually inside: an AC condensate line, a water heater, a washer valve or a supply hose. About 55 percent of homes were built before 1980, so older valves and heaters are common suspects.

If water is still spreading in Dana Point

Safety comes before salvage, especially in a storm.

1

Stop the source you control

Close the main valve on a pipe failure. Storm water you cannot stop, so move to the next steps.

2

Keep power away from the water

Turn off breakers only if the panel is dry and you do not have to cross water to reach it. Otherwise call 911 or your electric utility.

3

Go up, not out

Lift items from garage floors and low closets onto upper shelves. On bluff homes, avoid walking the slope while it is wet.

4

Treat flood and sewer water as contaminated

Water from creeks, street drains and toilets carries dirt and sewage. Keep children and pets out, and do not run a household vacuum on it.

5

Call the line
