Housing era

Newer than 1990 does not mean leak-proof

Aliso Viejo became a city on July 1, 2001. The city's housing element says more than two-thirds of its housing was built after 1990. Newer buildings mean fewer decades-old supply lines behind your walls, but appliances, water heaters and washer hoses fail at any age.

The same document says less than 28 percent of units may need maintenance or rehabilitation. So when water shows up in a newer unit, the cause is usually one failed part, such as a dishwasher line, an ice-maker tube or a water heater. A moisture scan finds that part's path and shows how far the water spread.

Condos, townhomes and detached homes leak in different ways

The housing element breaks the town into roughly 39 percent multifamily, 35 percent detached and 26 percent attached or townhouse. Each type sends water somewhere different, and each one changes who pays for what.

Apartments and condos

Water usually comes from the unit above or beside you. Ask the owner or manager to close the supply, then ask your association which pipes and walls it maintains.

Townhomes and attached homes

Shared walls and stacked floors let water drift into a neighbor's space. Tell your association early so both sides get dried.

Detached homes

A leak stays on your lot, so the scope and the rebuild are yours to manage. Check your policy before you approve any repair.

Read your CC&Rs before the walls come down

Aliso Viejo's housing element refers to homeowner associations, and attached homes usually sit under one. Your CC&Rs and the association's master policy decide which pipes, drywall and flooring are the association's and which are yours.

Ask your association before anyone cuts into a shared wall. Photograph the damage and keep every receipt.

If water is still spreading in Aliso Viejo

Move fast, but keep yourself safe. These five steps fit a condo, a townhome or a detached house, and none of them needs a tool you do not already own.

1

Close the nearest supply

Turn the angle stop under the sink, toilet or washer. If you cannot find the source, close the main shut-off for your unit or house.

2

Check the panel before the lights

Cut power to wet rooms only if the panel is dry and easy to reach. If water touches outlets or the panel, stay out and call 911 or your utility.

3

Clear the floor below the leak

Lift laptops, paper files and rugs off wet floors. Pull furniture away from walls so air can reach the baseboards.

4

Keep everyone out of sewage

Water from a toilet or floor drain is contaminated. Keep children and pets out of it and do not wade through it.

5
