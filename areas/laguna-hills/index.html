Biggest outdoor risk

Creek water and shallow groundwater

The city's Safety Element names Aliso Creek, Mill Creek, Oso Creek, Sulphur Creek, North Sulfur Creek and the La Paz Channel. It maps a FEMA 1 percent annual-chance floodplain around Aliso Creek, Veeh Reservoir and Mill Creek, and northwest of Alicia Parkway in the southern part of town. Lower-risk zones follow the La Paz Channel on the eastern border and North Sulfur Creek near Moulton Parkway.

Look up your address on the FEMA flood map before storm season. Standard homeowners policies usually exclude flood, so a mapped address is the moment to ask about separate flood coverage.

The same element reports groundwater as shallow as 5 to 10 feet near Sulphur, Aliso and Oso Creeks. Where the water table sits that high, rain can push moisture up through a slab or into a low garage. If water seeps in rather than pours in, tell the dispatcher, because the drying plan is different.

Plumbing from the 1970s and 1980s

Housing element tables show about 36.7 percent of Laguna Hills homes were built in 1970 to 1979 and 39.2 percent in 1980 to 1989. That is roughly three homes in four from those two decades. Some may still have original water heaters, shut-off valves or supply lines, so ask a licensed plumber what is worth replacing after a leak.

Most of those homes are detached (56.7 percent), but 18.2 percent of units are in buildings of five or more. A pipe failure in a stacked unit can reach a neighbor quickly, so shut the supply and tell your manager at once.

Slopes, reservoirs and what runs downhill

The Safety Element flags slopes steeper than 25 degrees as potentially unstable. It also says Veeh Reservoir and Rossmoor No. 1 Reservoir could cause local inundation or erosion if they failed. Note whether water enters at the base of a retaining wall or a graded slope.

Report that to the dispatcher. Ground water from outside is handled differently from a pipe leak inside.

When storm or pipe water is spreading across your floors

If water is still spreading in Laguna Hills

These steps fit a 1980s tract home as well as an upper-floor apartment.

1

Find the valve and close it

If the break is inside, shut the main at the front of the house or the unit's supply. If water is entering from outdoors, skip to the next step.

2

Power off, but only if dry

Switch off breakers for wet rooms only when you can reach a dry panel safely. Water at an outlet or panel means leave and call 911 or your utility.

3

Raise what you can

Put books, electronics and shoes on counters or beds. Roll up area rugs and prop sofa legs on foil or blocks.

4

Treat storm and sewage water as unsafe

Runoff from yards and drains can carry sewage and debris. Keep children and pets out, and wear boots if you must cross it.

5

Call the line

Call (949) 670-0939 and describe the source, the rooms and the depth. Do not wait for a callback before shutting off the source.
