Steep ground, canyons and where the water goes

Laguna Beach was incorporated in 1927, and much of it climbs steep, narrow, dead-end streets, with some neighborhoods on substandard lots. Rain on those slopes runs downhill, often into a garage or ground-floor room. The city's safety element calls flooding a recurring event here.

Laguna Canyon Channel and the storm drains

Most storm drains in Laguna Beach are sized for a 25-year storm and could be overwhelmed in a bigger one. The Boat Canyon and Bluebird Canyon drains handle a 100-year flood, though debris cuts that capacity. Laguna Canyon Channel can handle only 5 to 43 percent of a 100-year flood, depending on the point along it.

Near a canyon floor, a clogged inlet can send water into the street and then your door. Ask the city public works department who maintains the inlet on your block.

Slides and the Bluebird Canyon record

Bluebird Canyon had a landslide on October 2, 1978, and another large one in 2005. The city's safety element rates slopes on either side of Laguna, Bluebird and Aliso canyons as high or very high landslide risk.

A restoration crew dries and cleans what water has reached. It does not judge slope stability. If you see new wall cracks or mud at a wall base, step away and ask your city building department about a geotechnical review.

Salt air, sea level and rooms at beach height

The safety element flags Main Beach Park and lower levels near beach level as exposed, with beaches narrowing as sea level rises. Coastal moisture also slows drying, so wet materials need monitoring with moisture meters.

Find your FEMA flood zone by address. Flood is usually excluded from standard homeowners policies, so ask your insurer what you carry.

Water district and sewer questions

Laguna Beach County Water District serves portions of Laguna Beach, retail water only, and South Coast Water District serves South Laguna. We do not know parcel lines, so confirm your district by address.

For sewer backups, ask city public works who owns the line at your address. If sewage is on the floor, stay out and contact the utility first.

If water is still spreading in Laguna Beach

Mud, runoff and sewage count as contaminated water, so protect yourself first.

1

Stop the flow where you can

For a burst pipe, close the house shut-off valve. Slope runoff cannot be stopped, so keep it out of the doorway.

2

Power: panel first

Switch off breakers for wet rooms only if the panel is dry. A panel in a flooded garage is not safe to touch, so call 911 or the utility.

3

Raise what sits low

Pull items off garage and ground-floor floors and photograph the water line on the walls.

4

Treat mud and sewer water as hazardous

Keep children and pets out. Wear boots and gloves if you must cross it.

5

Call and describe your street

Dial (949) 670-0939 and give the source, wet rooms and whether the street is steep.

Laguna Beach housing and what it changes

About 71 percent of Laguna Beach homes are detached, about 12 percent are in buildings of two to four units and about 15 percent in buildings of five or more. Hillside detached homes often have a lower level or garage that takes the first water. In small multi-unit buildings, check with your owner or association before opening a ceiling.
