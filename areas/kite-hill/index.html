Kite Hill and Alicia Parkway

Neighborhood guides describe Kite Hill as a hilltop community of mostly single-family homes along Alicia Parkway, with a recreation center and clubhouse. They place most of the building in the 1980s. No official boundary map was found, so we describe the location in general terms.

The city's hazard plan says Alicia Parkway follows Aliso Creek, and that Aliso Creek only brushes the northwestern border of the city. Both facts are about roads and creeks. We found no source that says Kite Hill itself floods, and we do not suggest that it does.

Garages, slopes and rain

On a hilltop, rain that enters a garage or a lower room usually comes from a roof, a window seal, a drain or a door threshold. A restoration crew dries what the water reached. It does not redirect drainage or repair slopes.

After a heavy storm, look at ceilings, the tops of exterior walls and garage floors. A new stain that keeps growing means water is still entering. Call a roofer or a drainage professional for the source, and call us for the drying.

Check before you step in

Do not walk through standing water if outlets, extension cords or the panel are wet. Call 911 or your electric utility for that danger, and wait for qualified help.

Community rules and shared walls

A homeowners association governs many Kite Hill streets. Guides mention a recreation center and an association fee, but we did not confirm what the association requires for outside work. Ask your association whether it needs notice for work crews or equipment, and whether it keeps a list of approved vendors.

Drying is checked with moisture readings under the published ANSI/IICRC S500 reference. The EPA advises getting wet materials dry within 24 to 48 hours.

Local facts for your next step

Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status is not verified for Kite Hill. The city's Public Safety element says only a few places are in the 100-year zone and that the remainder has minimal flood risk, which is a citywide summary and not a map of your street. Enter your address in FEMA's Flood Map Service Center to see the zone that applies.

Moulton Niguel Water District's guide puts the house shut-off valve near the front of the home, below the hose bib. If you live in an attached unit, your association may control the main valve, so ask before you turn anything. OC Parks lists Aliso and Wood Canyons Wilderness Park at 28373 Alicia Parkway, about 4,500 acres, if you are asking where the open canyon land begins.

The city's environmental resources element splits electric service between Southern California Edison in the west and San Diego Gas & Electric in the east, with SoCalGas supplying gas. Check your bill to see which applies, and call 911 first for a gas smell.
