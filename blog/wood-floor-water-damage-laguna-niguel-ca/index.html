Water Damage to Wood Floors in Laguna Niguel: What to Do Fast

Wood takes on water quickly and gives it back slowly. What you do in the first two days decides whether the boards flatten out or have to be pulled.

HOMEOWNER GUIDEFIRST 48 HOURS
By the Laguna Niguel Water Damage Pros team | Published October 29, 2026
24/7 Emergency Line
(949) 670-0939

Get the water off the surface, stop the source, and start moving air across the floor right away. Wood absorbs moisture through its edges and underside, so a floor can look fine on top while the planks swell below. EPA guidance says to dry water-damaged areas within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold, and wood floors are one of the materials where that clock matters most.

Do not wait to see whether the boards bend. Cupping often shows up days later, after the worst chance to stop it has passed.

WOOD BASICS

What the water is doing to the planks

Wood is made of tiny tubes that swell as they fill. The bottom of a plank usually stays wetter than the top, because the underside sits against a subfloor or slab. When the underside swells faster than the face, the edges lift and the board curves.

Cupping

The edges of each board rise higher than the center, so the floor looks like a row of shallow troughs. This is the most common early sign and it can be reversible if the floor dries slowly and evenly.

Crowning

The center rises above the edges. It often appears after a cupped floor is sanded too early, before the boards have finished drying.

Buckling

Boards lift off the subfloor, sometimes in a ridge across the room. This is the severe stage, and the floor may need partial removal to relieve pressure.

Gaps and staining

As boards dry and shrink, gaps open between them. Dark stains at seams can point to trapped moisture or a dirty water source.

The first 48 hours, step by step

1

Stop the source and get the standing water out

Close the fixture valve or the house shut-off. Mop or use a wet vacuum on puddles, and remove rugs and furniture that hold water against the boards.

2

Photograph before you move anything

Take wide and close photos of the damage and the source. The California Department of Insurance advises photographs if possible, and your insurer will ask for them.

3

Start air movement and lower the humidity

Point fans across the floor, not at it, and run the air conditioning to pull moisture from the room. Household fans help only with surface moisture, so bring in trained crews if the floor has been wet more than a few hours.

4

Get a moisture reading on day one or two

A meter compares the wet floor to a dry reference board. Readings taken each day show whether the floor is drying toward normal or holding water underneath.

If water is under the planks, the floor often needs to be dried from below or through small openings. Crews use injection drying with mats or ports that pull moisture out of the cavity. This equipment needs training to run correctly, so we do not rent it out. If you are comparing options, see how emergency water removal handles the standing-water stage.

Do and do not, while you wait

On a wet wood floor

  • ✓Do remove wet rugs, mats and boxes, and wipe the surface dry.
  • ✓Do keep foot traffic off swollen boards, since they can split and snap.
  • ✓Do not use a heat gun, hair dryer or space heater to speed things up. Fast drying cracks and warps boards.
  • ✓Do not seal, wax or refinish until the floor reads dry across several days.
  • ✓Do not drill holes in the floor yourself. Injection ports are placed on a plan.
  • ✓Stay out if water reached outlets or a panel, or if sewage is involved, and call 911 or your utility first.

Wet wood floors in Laguna Niguel: rain season and slab homes

NOAA's 1991 to 2020 normals for the Laguna Beach station show most rain falling from November through March, with January near 2.8 inches and February near 3.4. Those are the months when roof leaks, door thresholds and window sills deliver water to floors. Check your seals in early fall and keep rugs back from exterior doors in winter.

Many Laguna Niguel homes sit on a concrete slab or a raised foundation rather than a basement. A wood floor over a slab can wick moisture from below as well as above, and a raised floor can trap it in the crawl space. Ask your inspector which type you have, because it changes where the crew needs to look.
