First hour

What to do while you wait for the crew

The first hour matters most, and most of it is simple. EPA guidance says to dry water-damaged areas and items within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth, so every minute you save now shortens the drying job later.

Safe steps before we arrive

  • ✓Stop the source. Your house shut-off valve is typically at the front of the home just below the hose bib, according to Moulton Niguel Water District. Toilets and sinks have their own stops.
  • ✓Turn off power to wet rooms only if the panel is dry and you can reach it safely. If water touches outlets or the panel, stay out and call 911 or your electric utility first.
  • ✓Smell gas? Leave the house and call SoCalGas and 911 before you call anyone else.
  • ✓Lift furniture legs onto foil or blocks, and move rugs, electronics and papers to a dry room.
  • ✓Keep people and pets out of any water that came from a drain, a toilet or a sewer backup.
  • ✓Take photos and short videos of the water, the source and the damaged items before anything moves.

Local conditions

Why water damage looks different in Laguna Niguel

Laguna Niguel sits in the San Joaquin Hills, and water here follows slopes, creeks and old plumbing. Knowing which one is at work tells you if the cause is a leak, a drainage problem or both.

Creeks and drains

The city says Sulphur Creek drains most of northern Laguna Niguel, running through Crown Valley Park and Laguna Niguel Regional Park, while the two forks of Salt Creek cross the south. Homes near those corridors should check the FEMA flood map for their address.

Most of the city is low flood risk

The city's safety element says the remainder of Laguna Niguel outside the mapped zones has minimal flood risk and that local flooding is usually tied to damaged or blocked drainage. In practice, a soaked room is more often a leak than a flood.

Older neighborhoods

The city's housing element counts about 6,000 homes built before 1980, with its older neighborhoods dating to the late 1960s and early 1970s. Original supply lines, water heaters and valves in those homes are the usual suspects.

Condos and townhomes

Roughly one in four homes in the city is multifamily. An upstairs unit that leaks sends water through a ceiling below, so documenting the damage and notifying your association early matters.

Rain still plays a part. NOAA's 1991 to 2020 normals for the Laguna Beach station show about 12.7 inches a year, with January and February the wettest months, and the city describes its rainfall as feast or famine. A dry year followed by a heavy one is when roof flashing, stucco cracks and clogged drains show their age.

Hillside homes deserve a second look after storms. The city's safety element maps a landslide zone category, so if your lot has a steep slope, ask the city building department what applies to your address before you repair anything structural.

Sewer service in Laguna Niguel is split

Moulton Niguel Water District supplies the water. Sewer service comes from Moulton Niguel or South Coast Water District depending on where you live, and the pipe from your house to the public sewer is the owner's responsibility per the city's sewage spill guide. If a drain backs up, stop using water, stay out of the area and call a plumber before you call us.

Insurance

Water damage and your insurance policy

Document first, then call your insurer

Sudden and accidental water damage, such as a burst pipe or a failed supply line, is often covered by a homeowners policy. Gradual leaks and neglect often are not, and flood damage is usually excluded and needs separate flood coverage. Check your policy or ask your insurer which applies to your loss.

California regulations require insurers to acknowledge a claim within 15 calendar days and to accept or deny it within 40 calendar days after proof of claim. We help by recording photos, moisture readings and a written scope that you can share with your adjuster. We never promise that a claim will be approved.
